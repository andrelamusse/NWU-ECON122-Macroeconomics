SECTION A / AFDELING A — MULTIPLE CHOICE
[200]
Choose the most correct answer. Each question carries 2 marks (100 × 2 = 200).
1.
Gross domestic product (GDP) is the total value of all ___ produced within the boundaries of a
country in a particular period.
A.  final goods and services
B.  final goods sold to households only
C.  goods and services produced by permanent residents, wherever they live
D.  goods and services, including intermediate goods
Answer: A — GDP counts only final goods and services to avoid double counting.
2.
Which one of the following would be included in South Africa's GDP for 2026?
A.  The purchase of existing shares on the JSE
B.  The sale of a 2019 second-hand car in 2026
C.  The salary of a South African engineer working in Dubai
D.  The value of a new house built in Polokwane during 2026
Answer: D — Only current production within SA's borders counts; resales and financial transactions are excluded.
3.
GDP at market prices is equal to GDP at factor cost plus ...
A.  depreciation (consumption of fixed capital)
B.  taxes on production and imports minus subsidies
C.  net primary income from the rest of the world
D.  subsidies minus taxes on production and imports
Answer: B — GDPmp = GDPfc + taxes - subsidies.
4.
GDP at factor cost is R4 800 billion, taxes on production and imports are R620 billion and subsidies
are R45 billion. GDP at market prices is:
A.  R4 135 billion
B.  R4 225 billion
C.  R5 465 billion
D.  R5 375 billion
Answer: D — 4 800 + 620 - 45 = R5 375 billion.
5.
Given C = R3 200 bn, I = R900 bn, G = R1 100 bn, X = R1 500 bn and Z = R1 450 bn, gross
domestic expenditure (GDE) is:
A.  R5 200 bn
B.  R3 750 bn
C.  R6 700 bn
D.  R5 250 bn
Answer: A — GDE = C + I + G = 3 200 + 900 + 1 100 = R5 200 bn (exports and imports are excluded).
6.
Which statement best explains why South Africa's gross national income (GNI) is usually smaller
than its GDP?
A.  South Africa imports more goods than it exports
B.  South Africa pays more primary income to the rest of the world than it receives
C.  Taxes on production exceed subsidies
D.  Depreciation of the capital stock is very large
Answer: B — GNI = GDP + primary income receipts - primary income payments; SA's payments exceed its
receipts.

7.
A farmer sells wheat for R10 000 to a miller, who sells flour for R12 500 to a baker, who sells bread
for R18 000 to a shopkeeper, who sells it to consumers for R21 000. The value added by the baker
is:
A.  R5 500
B.  R2 500
C.  R18 000
D.  R3 000
Answer: A — Value added = sales - purchases = 18 000 - 12 500 = R5 500.
8.
Real GDP measures the value of production ...
A.  at factor cost only
B.  after subtracting depreciation
C.  at current prices
D.  at constant (base-year) prices
Answer: D — Real GDP removes the effect of price changes by using base-year prices.
9.
Real GDP was R4 400 billion in 2025 and R4 532 billion in 2026. The real economic growth rate for
2026 is:
A.  2.91%
B.  3.00%
C.  13.20%
D.  30.00%
Answer: B — (4 532 - 4 400) / 4 400 x 100 = 3.00%.
10.
When GDP is calculated using the income approach, it is valued at:
A.  factor cost
B.  basic prices
C.  market prices
D.  constant prices
Answer: A — Income method = factor cost; production method = basic prices; expenditure method = market prices.
11.
When GDP is calculated using the production (value-added) approach, it is valued at:
A.  current exchange rates
B.  market prices
C.  factor cost
D.  basic prices
Answer: D — The production method uses basic prices.
12.
The further the Lorenz curve lies from the line of perfect equality (the diagonal), ...
A.  the lower the Gini coefficient
B.  the greater the inequality in the income distribution
C.  the more equal the income distribution
D.  the higher the per capita income
Answer: B — Distance from the diagonal measures inequality.
13.
A Gini coefficient of 0 indicates ...
A.  that the Lorenz curve lies on the horizontal axis
B.  perfect equality
C.  perfect inequality
D.  that one household earns all income
Answer: B — Gini ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality).
14.
The quantile ratio as a measure of inequality compares ...
A.  the area between the diagonal and the Lorenz curve with the total area
B.  nominal GDP with real GDP
C.  the income share of the richest group with that of the poorest group
D.  the number of employed with the labour force
Answer: C — E.g. income share of top 10% / bottom 10%.

15.
The current account of the balance of payments records ...
A.  only changes in gold and foreign reserves
B.  flows of shares, bonds and direct investment
C.  only unrecorded transactions (errors and omissions)

Which one of the following is NOT one of the five main macroeconomic objectives?
A.  Maximising government tax revenue
B.  Full employment
C.  Price stability
D.  Equitable distribution of income
Answer: A — The five objectives: growth, full employment, price stability, BoP stability, equitable income

A shop labels a loaf of bread at R19.99. Here money is performing its function as a ...
A.  medium of exchange
B.  unit of account
C.  store of value
D.  standard of deferred payment only
Answer: B — Prices are expressed in money: unit of account.
18.
Thabo keeps R5 000 in a savings account to spend next year. Money is serving as a ...
A.  medium of exchange
B.  factor of production
C.  unit of account
D.  store of value
Answer: D — Holding purchasing power over time = store of value.
19.
High inflation most directly undermines money's function as a ...
A.  means of barter
B.  store of value
C.  unit of account
D.  medium of exchange
Answer: B — Inflation erodes the purchasing power of stored money.
20.
M1 in South Africa consists of ...
A.  M2 plus long-term deposits
B.  coins and banknotes in circulation plus demand deposits of the domestic private sector
C.  coins and banknotes only
D.  all short- and medium-term deposits
Answer: B — M1 = C + D (narrowest measure, medium of exchange).
21.
M3, the most comprehensive measure of money, is equal to ...
A.  coins and notes plus cheque deposits
B.  M2 minus quasi-money
C.  M2 plus long-term deposits (longer than six months)
D.  M1 plus short- and medium-term deposits
Answer: C — M3 = M2 + long-term deposits.
22.
Coins and notes in circulation = R50 m, demand deposits = R300 m, short- and medium-term
deposits = R400 m and long-term deposits = R250 m. M2 is:
A.  R1 000 m
B.  R650 m
C.  R350 m
D.  R750 m
Answer: D — M1 = 350; M2 = 350 + 400 = R750 m.

23.
The demand for active balances (transactions and precautionary motives) depends mainly on ...
A.  expected bond prices only
B.  the level of income
C.  the interest rate
D.  the exchange rate
Answer: B — Active balances (L1) are a function of income.
24.
The speculative demand for money (passive balances) is ...
A.  positively related to the interest rate
B.  inversely related to the interest rate
C.  independent of the interest rate
D.  determined only by the level of income
Answer: B — Higher interest = higher opportunity cost of holding money.
25.
An increase in national income will, ceteris paribus, ...
A.  have no effect on the demand for money
B.  cause a movement up along LL
C.  shift the total demand for money curve (LL) to the right
D.  shift LL to the left
Answer: C — Higher income raises demand for active balances, shifting LL right.
26.
If the interest rate increases from 7% to 8%, ceteris paribus, there will be ...
A.  a movement along LL to a smaller quantity of money demanded
B.  a leftward shift of LL
C.  a rightward shift of LL
D.  an increase in the quantity of money demanded
Answer: A — A change in i causes a movement along the curve, not a shift.
27.
In the South African approach to the money supply, the quantity of money is determined by ...
A.  the SARB printing a fixed amount of banknotes
B.  the demand for money and the interest rate (there is no independent money supply curve)
C.  the amount of gold held by the SARB
D.  the government budget deficit only
Answer: B — Money is demand-determined (endogenous); SARB influences it via the repo rate.
28.
The main instrument of monetary policy used by the SARB is the ...
A.  personal income tax rate
B.  repo rate
C.  VAT rate
D.  import tariff
Answer: B — Accommodation policy through the repo rate.
29.
When the SARB sells government bonds in the open market, it ...
A.  lowers the repo rate automatically
B.  increases banks' cash reserves
C.  reduces banks' cash reserves and enlarges the money-market shortage
D.  increases the money supply
Answer: C — Selling bonds drains liquidity.
30.
Which one of the following is NOT a function of the South African Reserve Bank?
A.  Acting as banker and adviser to the government
B.  Acting as lender of last resort to banks
C.  Collecting personal income tax from individuals
D.  Formulating and implementing monetary policy
Answer: C — Tax collection is SARS's function.

Public goods are goods that are ...
A.  non-rival but excludable

The free-rider problem associated with public goods arises mainly because public goods are ...
A.  rival in consumption
B.  subject to VAT
C.  non-excludable

Fish in the open ocean are an example of a ...
A.  natural monopoly
B.  pure public good
C.  merit good

When a factory pollutes a river, ...
A.  the good is underproduced
B.  marginal social cost exceeds marginal private cost and the good is overproduced
C.  marginal private cost exceeds marginal social cost
D.  social benefit exceeds private benefit
Answer: B — Negative externality: MSC > MPC, market output too high.
36.
The Coase theorem states that ...
A.  government must always tax polluters
B.  if the parties to an externality can bargain, they will reach an efficient outcome without
government intervention
C.  monopolies should be nationalised
D.  public goods must be provided by the state
Answer: B — Assumptions rarely hold in practice.
37.
To achieve the socially efficient output, a tax on a polluting industry should equal ...
A.  the marginal private cost
B.  the VAT rate
C.  the firm's total profit
D.  the difference between marginal social cost and marginal private cost
Answer: D — This internalises the externality.
38.
A group of firms lobbies politicians for protection so that they earn higher profits at the expense of
society. This is an example of ...
A.  horizontal equity
B.  the benefit principle
C.  rent seeking
D.  a positive externality
Answer: C — Rent seeking is a cause of government failure.
